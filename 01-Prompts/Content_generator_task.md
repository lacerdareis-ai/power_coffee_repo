## EARNED-MEDIA RULES (added 2026-08-14, from a food & beverage editor panel)

These came from national food editors (The Kitchn, Delish, Good Housekeeping, Parents,
Modern Retail) describing how they decide what to cover, via notes shared on a startup
CPG group email list.

SOURCING GUARD: Power Coffee did NOT host or attend this panel. Never write copy that
claims or implies otherwise — no "we sat down with five editors", no "when we asked
them", no "our media panel". Use the rules to shape how content is written; do not make
the panel itself the subject of a post.

Three of the five apply directly to on-platform content, not just to press pitches:

1. **Stories, not products.** "If you just tell me you're launching a new flavor, I have
   to do a lot of heavy lifting to get that into a story." Never open on the SKU, the
   flavor or the ingredient. Open on the angle. This is the same instruction as the
   Desire-Brand rule — treat a post the way an editor treats a pitch: if the reader has
   to do the work to find why it matters, they scroll.

2. **A single item is not news; a trend is.** One SKU rarely earns coverage, but
   roundups run constantly. Bucket content into the larger moment it belongs to
   (functional coffee, the afternoon crash, no-crash energy) rather than presenting the
   product as a standalone event. Naming the wider category is a strength, not a leak.

3. **Discovery moved and trends expire in days.** TikTok is the engine, with Reddit
   threads and LinkedIn founder journeys alongside it. A trend that popped last week may
   already be stale. Content that hooks a live trend must ship inside its window —
   prefer this week's tension over an evergreen restatement.
