## SCIENCE NOTE — Founder Interview Prep (not for publishing)
**Ingredient / mechanism:** Taurine as an osmolyte and cytoprotective modulator

Taurine is one of the most abundant free amino acids in excitable tissue (skeletal muscle, heart, CNS), yet it isn't incorporated into proteins — it exists as a free intracellular osmolyte. Its primary mechanistic roles: (1) cell volume regulation, buffering osmotic stress during metabolic exertion; (2) modulation of intracellular calcium handling in cardiac and skeletal muscle, which influences contractile function and fatigue resistance; (3) conjugation with bile acids in the liver; and (4) a proposed role as a weak agonist at GABA-A and glycine receptors in the CNS, distinct from classical stimulant action, which may explain why high-dose taurine does not produce the same subjective "wired" sensation as caffeine alone. At the 2.1g serving level in Power Coffee, taurine's role is best framed as metabolic support for sustained physical output rather than a direct CNS stimulant pathway.

**Reference:** Ripps H, Shen W. "Review: Taurine: A 'very essential' amino acid." Mol Vis. 2012;18:2673-2686. PMID: 23170060

**Interview angle:** Taurine doesn't create energy — it supports the cellular conditions that let the body use energy efficiently under stress. Framed as support, never as a stimulant replacement or guarantee.
