### Body
## The 5-Minute Window Most People Give Away

Before the notifications. Before the inbox. Before anyone asks anything of you — there's a window.

It's short. Usually five to ten minutes between when you wake up and when the day's demands start generating their own momentum. Most people fill it reflexively: phone in hand, scrolling before they're fully awake.

The people who consistently perform at a high level tend to treat that window differently. Not because they're more disciplined — because they've built a trigger.

## What Behavioral Anchoring Actually Means

Behavioral anchoring is a well-documented pattern in habit and performance research. The core finding: a consistent, intentional sequence before a task trains the brain to shift into a focused state faster and hold it longer.

The sequence itself becomes the signal. Your nervous system learns the cue — *this is when we focus* — and begins preparing before the primary task even starts.

It doesn't require a complex routine. A specific mug. A fixed order of steps. Five minutes of quiet before the phone comes up. The consistency is the mechanism, not the elaborateness.

## Why the Ritual Matters Before the Formula

Most conversations about morning energy start with what you consume. Caffeine. Protein. Hydration. These things matter — but they're downstream of the ritual itself.

A cup of coffee taken reactively, mid-scroll, in response to the first notification of the day, hits differently than the same cup taken intentionally before the day has made any demands.

The ritual primes the neurological state. The formula sustains it.

## Building the Formula Into the Ritual

This is why Power Coffee was designed the way it was. The 11-ingredient formula — caffeine paired with L-theanine for a clean focus shift, taurine for output that holds past hour three, ginkgo biloba for sustained cerebral clarity, pea protein for blood sugar stability — was built to carry the intention of the ritual forward into the actual work.

The L-theanine moderates the caffeine response so the shift is smooth, not spiked. The taurine extends the output window. The matcha adds a secondary, slower curve. Together, they make the ritual's promise easier to keep.

## The Audit Worth Running

Saturday morning is a low-pressure moment to notice what your morning actually looks like versus what you'd design it to look like if you were starting from scratch.

Most people find a gap. That gap is where the friction lives — and where the ritual, consistently applied, tends to close it.

Start with five minutes. Same time, same sequence, same intention. The compound effect of that consistency tends to show up within two to three weeks — not as a dramatic shift, but as a quieter, more reliable version of your best focus.

The window is yours. It's just a matter of choosing to use it.
