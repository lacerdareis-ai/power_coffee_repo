You slept fine. You had your coffee. And yet by 11am you're staring at your screen wondering where your focus went.

## What Caffeine Does (and Doesn't Do)

Caffeine works by blocking adenosine receptors — the compounds responsible for making you feel sleepy. That blockade produces the familiar feeling of alertness and reduced fatigue.

The problem is, caffeine does this alone. There's no compound buffering the cortisol spike it triggers. No mechanism for extending the alertness window. No support for the downstream cognitive work that follows.

So the first 60-90 minutes feel great. Then the cortisol dip hits, adenosine starts flooding back, and you're left reaching for another cup that will do the same thing again — just less effectively.

## The Stack That Actually Holds

The research on caffeine combined with L-theanine is among the most consistent in cognitive performance science. L-theanine, a compound found naturally in green tea, modulates the caffeine response: same alertness, smoother curve, longer duration, no hard crash.

Add taurine — an amino acid that supports sustained cardiovascular and muscular output — and you extend the window further. Add ginkgo biloba, which has been studied for its role in cerebral blood flow, and you're supporting the cognitive infrastructure, not just stimulating it.
