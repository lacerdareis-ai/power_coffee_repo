## Source: Euromonitor — "From Cravings to Conscious Choices: The Rise of Intentional Snacking"
By Rasa Vaiciuniene & Egle Tekutyte, published 1/15/2026

**Citable stats (verbatim, do not alter):**
- 51% of global consumers actively seek healthy ingredients in food and beverages. (Euromonitor Voice of the Consumer: Lifestyles Survey, fielded Jan–Feb 2025)
- 37% of global consumers carefully read nutrition labels. (same survey)
- 21% of global consumers eat nutrient-rich snacks that complement their diet. (same survey)
- Gluten-free claims lead new-launch growth in Health & Wellness snacks 2021–2025, ahead of vegetarian claims. (Euromonitor Health and Wellness Snacks data)
- Top consumer priorities when choosing a health/wellness product feature: feeling healthier, supporting immunity, maintaining digestive health. (Euromonitor Voice of the Consumer: Health and Nutrition Survey, fielded Feb 2025)
- Gut health is positioned as a hub connecting brain, immunity, skin, metabolism, and reproductive health in upcoming category focus.

**Named examples from the article** (real brands, safe to reference for context, not as if we're endorsed by them):
- Biogena Moments — snack bites claiming relaxation/sleep/immunity/beauty support.
- Evelyn's The PMS Bar — protein + fibre + L-tryptophan/magnesium/B6 for menstrual-cycle support.
- Marks & Spencer "Only Ingredients" range (Ocado) — simplified formulations, no added sugar, higher protein/fibre.

**Core thesis of the piece:** consumers now want sensory appeal (taste/texture) *and* a functional benefit in the same product — novelty alone doesn't sustain a launch, better-for-you formulation + convenience + a clear use-occasion does.

## How this maps to Power Coffee (suggested angles, not final copy)

1. **"51% actively seek healthy ingredients" → ingredient transparency post.** Power Coffee already lists all 8 active ingredients on the bag (caffeine, matcha, taurine, L-theanine, pea protein, ginkgo, cinnamon, ginger). Contrast: most coffee brands still just say "coffee." This stat gives the "why we list every ingredient" post a real, external number instead of a founder-only claim.

2. **"37% read nutrition labels" + "21% eat nutrient-rich snacks that complement their diet" → the gap post.** Frame: most people who read the label still end up with a coffee that has one active ingredient (caffeine). Power Coffee's formula answers what the *other* 79% are missing.

3. **"Consumers want sensory appeal AND function, not novelty alone" → direct validation of the existing brand thesis** ("Tastes Like Coffee. Hits Like It Should."). Can open a caption with this exact framing: it's not us saying it, it's Euromonitor's Jan 2026 report on snacking.

4. **Gluten-free/protein/fibre claims outperforming → supports the pea-protein ingredient story.** Useful for a differentiation-pillar post about why protein is in the formula at all (blood sugar stability), tied to broader category data instead of only founder anecdote.

Do not stretch this into "gut health" claims for Power Coffee specifically unless the formula actually targets gut health — the ginger/matcha ingredients are not FDA-reviewed for that, and the no-ai-slop rule against fabricated/overclaimed benefits applies here same as anywhere else.
