## SCHEDULED THIS WEEK (5)

### Post 1 — "Deliverability is shared" (seed 1) → Oct 20
Your email list's reputation isn't per-address. It's shared.

One bad batch of non-opted-in addresses doesn't just risk itself — it risks the deliverability of everyone who actually signed up, because every inbox provider scores your whole sending domain as one thing.

"Grow the list" and "protect the list" are not the same move.

### Post 2 — "Two questions, not one" (seed 2) → Oct 21
Every list-growth decision is actually two decisions wearing one trenchcoat: is this list worth having, and is it safe to add right now.

We almost treated 490 new addresses as a single yes/no. It's two separate questions — and conflating them is exactly how a growing list quietly becomes an unsendable one.

**IG treatment:** Quote card, same system, variant layout (pull-quote style, brown accent divider).

### Post 3 — "Not every exclusion is a judgment call" (seed 3) → Oct 22
Not every exclusion is a judgment call.

Out of roughly 490 new addresses, two got flagged for removal — and neither one needed a debate. One was an automated bounce handler. One was another company's own unsubscribe address. Routing errors, not contacts.

Knowing the difference between "decide carefully" and "obviously not a person" keeps you from treating easy calls like hard ones.

**IG treatment:** Quote card, numbered-list visual treatment (2 excluded / 488 added) for scannability.

### Post 4 — "Simple systems trade flexibility for forgiveness" (seed 4) → Oct 23
We run our weekly email off one flat file. No segmentation, no per-send review — Saturday comes, everyone in the file gets the email.

That's efficient. It's also unforgiving: there's no safety net between a bad edit to that file and real inboxes on the other end.

Simple systems trade flexibility for forgiveness. Know which one you're buying.

**IG treatment:** Quote card, same system.

---

### Post 5 — "The real question before you hit save" (closing synthesis) → Oct 24
Growing to 8,498 addresses felt like a milestone. It almost cost us the deliverability of the other 8,006.

Not because the new addresses were bad people. Because "more" and "safe" pull in different directions more often than the first instinct admits.

The question before you hit save isn't "will this grow the list." It's "will this still be a list people open after I do."
