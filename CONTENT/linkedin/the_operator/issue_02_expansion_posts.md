## SCHEDULED THIS WEEK (5)

### Tue — Post 2 — Seed: the adenosine mechanism

The afternoon crash isn't a willpower problem. It's a receptor clearing.

Caffeine blocks your brain from reading a buildup signal called adenosine
for a few hours. It doesn't remove the buildup. When the block wears off,
the signal comes back — often stronger than before you started.

**IG treatment:** quote card, navy/cream system: "The crash isn't
willpower. It's a receptor clearing."

### Wed — Post 3 — Seed: you're not one person all day

You are not the same decision-maker at 10am and 4pm.

Same person, same calendar, genuinely different capacity — and most of us
let whatever's open on the calendar decide which version handles the hard
call, instead of deciding on purpose.

I scheduled the hardest conversation of a week for 4:47pm once, by
accident. I've since started treating "who's actually available right
now" as a real question, not just "what's open."

**IG treatment:** carousel, 2 slides — slide 1 the hook, slide 2 the
4:47pm callback as a closing line.

### Thu — Post 4 — Seed: you're not one person all day

Sharp at 10am. Running on fumes at 4pm. Same person, same day — two
different decision-makers.

Most calendars don't know the difference. Yours should.

**IG treatment:** quote card, two-line format, no photo needed.

### Fri — Post 5 — Seed: audit your calendar

Try this: look at your calendar for the last two weeks. Find every
genuinely hard conversation — a hire, a fire, a hard no, a real
negotiation. Check what hour each one actually happened at, not what hour
you'd have picked on purpose.

I did this after a Thursday I'd rather not repeat. Almost everything hard
had drifted into late afternoon, because that's when the backlog finally
cleared enough to deal with it. Not because it was the right hour. Because
it was the only open one.

The fix isn't a new app. It's noticing the pattern once.

**IG treatment:** caption-led, founder photo, the ask ("try this") carries
the post — good engagement-bait, lean into it in the caption CTA.

### Sat — Post 6 — Seed: willpower vs. a receptor

"Push through it" is advice for a willpower problem.

The 4pm wall isn't a willpower problem. It's a receptor doing exactly
what it's supposed to do, on schedule, whether or not your calendar
agrees.

You can't will your way past a mechanism. You can only know where you
are on it before you schedule something that actually matters.

**IG treatment:** quote card, closing-of-the-week tone, pairs naturally
with Tuesday's post as a bookend.
