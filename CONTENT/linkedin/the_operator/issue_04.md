# I tried to launch three things in one week. One of them worked.

Not because I ran out of time. Because I ran out of shape.

The plan looked fine on a calendar: ship the priority thing Monday through Wednesday, then use the back half of the week for the other two. On paper, same total hours either way. In practice, the first thing got real attention — rested mornings, full focus — and shipped clean. The other two got whatever was left after 9pm, after the tank was already empty. Both needed real rework days later. One of them twice.

I used to think that was a willpower problem. Push harder, sleep less, it evens out. It doesn't, and there's a clean mechanism for why that isn't just a vibe.

Regular caffeine hits fast and clears in about five hours. All stimulant, no shape — it spikes, it works, and then it's just gone, often sharper on the way down than you'd like. The research on pairing caffeine with L-theanine is specifically about what happens when you change the shape of that same input: studies on the combination show measurably steadier attention and fewer of the rough edges — tiredness, headaches — that show up with caffeine running alone. Not more stimulant. The same stimulant, shaped differently, held longer, landing softer.

That's exactly where my three-things week went wrong, and it had nothing to do with effort. I put the same total hours into the week either way. What changed was the shape — one sustained push on the thing that mattered, versus three things getting crammed into whatever hours were left, unshaped, back-loaded, no recovery between them.

A hustle problem usually isn't a hustle problem. It's a shape problem wearing a hustle costume. The fix was never "do less." It was: protect one thing at a time long enough for it to actually land clean, instead of spreading the same total effort so thin across three things that none of them get a real arc.

I changed one thing after that week: nothing new gets stacked onto a launch until the thing already in motion has actually landed — shipped, checked, done — not just "mostly there." Slower by the calendar. Fewer rework days by the month.

Most performance advice tells you to manage your energy. The actual lever, most weeks, is managing the shape you put it into.
