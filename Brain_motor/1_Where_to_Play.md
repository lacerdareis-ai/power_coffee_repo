## 4. Operation Conditions

### Main Regulations

> Navigating evolving U.S. supplement regulations is key for trust-building and long-term brand credibility.

**FDA (Food and Drug Administration):**

- Supplements are regulated under DSHEA (1994), not approved before market, but companies are responsible for safety and labeling.
- Ingredients like caffeine, L-theanine, ginkgo, and taurine must be from GRAS (Generally Recognized as Safe) lists or have sufficient data.

**FTC (Federal Trade Commission):**

- Claims related to focus, energy, metabolism, etc. must be truthful, not misleading, and backed by reliable scientific evidence.
- Influencer endorsements and testimonials must disclose paid partnerships.

**Threats and Opportunities:**

- *Opportunity:* Use "plant-based," "no artificial ingredients," and "made in the U.S." as credibility builders. Consider NSF or Informed-Sport certifications.
- *Threat:* Rising legal actions against unverified claims by influencers. Potential delays in marketing rollouts due to label review.

### Certifications

| Certification | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| FDA Compliance (DSHEA) | Mandatory | Follows U.S. supplement regulations for safety, labeling, and structure-function claims |
| COA (Certificate of Analysis) | Voluntary (strongly recommended) | 3rd-party lab results confirming ingredient purity, safety, and dosage per batch |
| NSF Certified for Sport® | Voluntary | Ensures product is free of banned substances; trusted in fitness, collegiate, and pro sports markets |
| USDA Organic (if applicable) | Voluntary | Strong trust mark for health-conscious buyers; requires full supply chain compliance |
