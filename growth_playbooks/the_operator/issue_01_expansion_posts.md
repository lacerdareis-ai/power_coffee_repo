### Wed — Post 4 — Seed: subtraction, not a better prompt

The best fix I shipped this month wasn't a feature. It was a deletion.

A product photo kept rendering our packaging slightly wrong, no matter how
precisely I described it. So I stopped describing the packaging. Built
the same scene with it out of frame entirely.

Nothing to get wrong is a more reliable fix than "try to get it less
wrong."

**IG treatment:** founder photo or simple graphic, caption-led, no
carousel needed.

### Thu — Post 6 — Seed: looks right vs. is right

A photo can be completely legible and completely wrong at the same time.

We learned that the expensive way — a product image that read perfectly
clean and showed the wrong product.

Legible was never the bar. We just hadn't written down what the real bar
was until it got crossed.

**IG treatment:** quote card, this single line as the centerpiece:
"Legible was never the bar."

### Fri — Post 1 — Seed: garbled vs. wrong

We caught fifteen AI-generated product photos with garbled fake text on
the packaging. Obvious once you looked. Easy to catch.

The dangerous ones came after.

Two more photos passed that first check clean — because the text was
perfectly legible. It just said the wrong thing. A wrong ingredient. A
wrong number.

Garbled text fails a glance test. Wrong-but-legible text passes it. If
your QA process is "does this look broken," you're only catching half the
problem.

**IG treatment:** carousel, 3 slides — slide 1 the hook line, slide 2 the
"two more passed clean" turn, slide 3 the closing line as a quote card.

---

### Sat — Post 7 — Seed: what actually changed

After the photo audit, we went from 24 usable product images to 8.

That's not a small cut for a lean team. It meant real constraints on what
content we could build for weeks — fewer scenarios, more reused photos,
harder creative problems.

We kept the 8 instead of patching the 16 we lost. A smaller, verified pool
beats a bigger pool you can't fully trust. Even when the smaller pool
costs you content you wanted to make.

**IG treatment:** text card, the "24 → 8" framing again as a closing
callback to Tuesday's post — deliberate repetition, not a mistake.
