# Visual Winning Pattern System — Images & Video

Added 2026-09-26. Synthesizes `04-Analytics/Winning-Patterns.md`'s own proven
data with external DTC creative research into one production rule set for
what actually gets generated/filmed, not just what copy says. Companion to
`daily_content/_brand_kit/swipe_file/README.md` (the raw reference material)
and the mascot-focused `First Win, Everywhere` strategy — this doc is the
photography/video half of "what converts," not the mascot half.

## The one finding that should change the weekly plan

**Reels out-reach every other format by 8-15x, every time it's been
measured, for months.** Not a one-off spike — Jun (199 vs 86), Jun 22 (416
vs 51), Jul 20 (+336%), Aug 3 (+1,712%). If a week's production time is
limited, a Reel is worth more than any other single asset that could be made
with the same hours. This isn't a preference — it's the highest-confidence,
most-repeated finding in the account's own history.

## What kind of video, specifically

Not just "more Reels" — the internal data names the exact winning format:
**unscripted, founder-to-camera, with the rawness stated up front.** "I
didn't write a script for this one" is the single best-performing post in
the account's history (526 reach, 18 comments, 16.2% ER — a period high on
every metric simultaneously). External research independently confirms the
same mechanism at scale: UGC-style creative beats polished production by
3-5x on conversion, CPM, and ROAS across 400+ DTC brands studied. Two
unrelated sources, same answer — this is about as confident as creative
guidance gets.

**Production implication:** the instinct to make a founder video "better" by
scripting it, re-shooting it, or cutting it tighter is very likely working
against the format. The imperfection is the trust signal, not a flaw to fix
in post. Film in one take where possible. Say out loud that it's unscripted
if it is — that naming is itself part of what performs, not just the raw
footage.

## What kind of photo, specifically

Two internal findings plus external data point the same direction:

1. **Real-world event photos are an automatic account-high**, unedited, no
   caption optimization, posted within 24 hours. This is the cheapest
   possible win available to the account and it's already proven — the only
   failure mode is not shipping the photo fast enough.
2. **Product photography should be hybrid, not one style.** External data:
   white-background/clean shots win as the fast, clear "what is this"
   image; lifestyle photography wins everywhere context and desire matter —
   ads, social, the rest of a carousel. Don't pick one register for
   everything; match the photo type to the job the slide is doing (a hook
   slide sells a feeling — lifestyle; a spec/ingredient callout sells
   clarity — clean product shot).
3. **The untested taxonomy is worth closing.** The account has posted across
   six visual categories (marble product, lifestyle kitchen, UGC-phone,
   ingredient flatlay, before/after split, text-on-solid) without ever
   tagging which one actually drove the reach/ER on each post. Start tagging
   `visual_style: <category>` in each day's content.md going forward so this
   stops being a blind spot — six months from now this becomes as strong a
   signal as the Reels-vs-Feed finding already is.

## The gap nobody's fixing: saves

Saves sit near zero across the entire account, on every format. That's not
"this content isn't save-worthy" so much as "nothing has been built with
saving in mind" — a scroll-through narrative carousel isn't the same object
as a reference chart someone bookmarks for later. Concretely: at least one
carousel a week should be structured as something with standalone reference
value after the scroll ends (a decision checklist, an ingredient-dose
comparison table, a "what to look for on a label" guide) rather than a story
arc. This is a real, currently-empty opportunity, not a hypothesis.

## Platform is not a resize — it's a different creative

The same visual solution does not transfer across platforms even when the
underlying claim is identical. Proven case: P5 Social Proof as a black-card
cream-quote image works on Instagram (65 reach, a top post) and dies on
LinkedIn (63 impressions, 0 engagements) with the exact same headline. The
fix already logged: LinkedIn needs the identical proof point delivered as a
story ("A review stopped me mid-scroll on Tuesday") instead of a headline
card. Before reusing one asset across platforms, ask what format that
platform's own audience already responds to — don't assume a resize is
enough.

## What to avoid

- **Negative product-category hooks.** "YOUR COFFEE IS MAKING YOU ANXIOUS"
  underperformed the carousel average by more than half. A hook (visual or
  copy) that attacks the category the viewer already belongs to (coffee
  drinkers) makes them defensive before the payoff arrives. Attack the
  *unsupported version* of the category, never the reader's existing habit.
- **Scripting/polishing founder video past the point of feeling real.** See
  above — this is the single highest-confidence "don't" in the whole
  system.
- **One creative style for every job.** The strongest accounts (internally
  and externally) match the register to the moment: raw for founder trust,
  clean for spec clarity, real-event for community proof, platform-specific
  for LinkedIn vs Instagram. A single house style applied everywhere is
  exactly what both the internal saves-gap and the LinkedIn/Instagram
  mismatch are symptoms of.
