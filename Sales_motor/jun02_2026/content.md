### Body
## Taurine Has a PR Problem

Most people associate taurine with energy drinks. That association isn't wrong — it is just incomplete. Taurine appears in energy drinks because it works. The problem is that most of those products use it at doses too low to produce the effects the research demonstrates.

Understanding what taurine actually does at the cellular level changes the way you evaluate any functional product that includes it.

## Taurine Is a Mitochondrial Support Compound

Your mitochondria are the organelles inside your cells responsible for producing ATP — adenosine triphosphate, the molecule your body uses as direct energy currency. The quality of your energy, including how sustained it feels, how cleanly it runs, and how well it holds up under cognitive or physical stress, depends directly on mitochondrial function.

Taurine supports this system through two primary mechanisms.

First, it regulates intracellular calcium. When calcium signaling inside cells becomes dysregulated — which happens under oxidative stress, sleep deprivation, or prolonged high-output work — mitochondrial membrane potential drops and ATP production degrades. Taurine acts as a stabilizing agent for this process, helping maintain the electrochemical gradient that drives efficient energy production.

Second, taurine functions as a direct antioxidant inside the mitochondrial membrane. Oxidative damage to mitochondria accumulates over time, compounding during demanding periods. Taurine reduces this damage, preserving the infrastructure that your cells depend on to produce clean output.

## Why the Dose Changes Everything

The clinical literature on taurine's mitochondrial effects concentrates on doses of one to two grams. Studies examining taurine's impact on oxidative stress, cellular energy, and cognitive output have generally used this range.

Most commercial products that include taurine use 200 to 500 milligrams. At those amounts, taurine can appear prominently on an ingredient label without meaningfully replicating what the research studied. This practice — known as ingredient dusting — is common in the supplement industry and difficult for consumers to identify without knowing what to look for.

The Power Coffee uses 2 grams of taurine per serving. Not because it is a larger number, but because that is the dose the evidence supports.

## What This Feels Like in Practice

Taurine's effect is not felt as a stimulant. You will not notice it the way you notice caffeine.

What you notice is its absence of the typical drop. Your energy baseline stays higher into the afternoon. The output does not fall off a cliff at noon. Your cognitive performance holds up through the second and third meetings of the day, not just the first.

That is the difference between energy that ignites and energy that sustains — and taurine at the right dose is a significant part of the mechanism behind the latter.

If your current morning routine is getting you to 10am but not to 2pm, the ingredient stack — and its doses — are worth examining.
