### Specs
- Format: 150g resealable pack, ~30 servings
- Price: $21.90/pack — $0.73/serving
- No sugar, no artificial sweeteners, no fillers, lactose-free
- Can be used standalone or added to existing coffee ritual

### Science hooks (for content)
- "Matcha + L-Theanine: calm focus without spike — energy over 4-6 hours, not 45 minutes"
- "Ginkgo Biloba: supports cerebral blood flow. Used for centuries. Now backed by research."
- "Cinnamon: blood sugar stability changes focus quality, not just energy level"
- "Taurine: neuromodulator, not a stimulant. Calms overstimulation. 2g per serving."
- "Pea protein: stabilizes blood sugar so energy doesn't spike and crash. 5g per scoop."

### Ingredient Hook Performance — LENS Data (May 4–22, 2026)
| Ingredient | Hook style | ER | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ginkgo Biloba | "The tree that survived a nuclear blast" (surprising historical claim) | **10.53%** | Best ER in SM period. Lead with claim, not ingredient name. |
| Pea Protein | "THERE'S PROTEIN IN YOUR COFFEE" (ingredient name first) | **2.22%** | Underperformed 4.7x vs Ginkgo. Direct name hook triggers skepticism. |
**Rule confirmed by data:** Never lead with the ingredient name. Lead with the mechanism, history, or the pain it solves. The ingredient is the payoff, not the hook.

### Positioning statement
> "They say quit coffee. I say upgrade it."
> Power Coffee is for people who already take their mornings seriously.

### What NOT to claim (FTC/FDA compliance)
- No medical claims (does not treat/cure/prevent any disease)
- No "clinically proven" unless specific studies cited
- No "boosts testosterone" or hormonal claims
- No guaranteed outcomes ("will", "always", "100%")
- Safe language: "supports", "helps", "designed to", "may", "associated with", "can be part of"
- Add disclaimer where appropriate: "Not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease."

## Target Audience

### Primary Segments
| Segment | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| High-performance professionals | Executives, managers, remote workers needing sustained focus |
| Entrepreneurs & founders | Building companies, need energy without burnout |
| Morning ritual people | Already have a routine — looking to upgrade it |
| Fitness-oriented adults 25-45 | Train regularly, care about what goes in their body |
| Biohacking enthusiasts | Seek science-backed edge in performance |

### Core Pain Points
1. **The afternoon crash** — regular coffee gives energy then steals it
2. **Jitters** — anxious, wired, but not focused
3. **Mental fog** — 3 coffees and still can't think clearly
4. **Time pressure** — no time for complex morning rituals
5. **Trust problem** — sick of supplements that overpromise

### Proven Conversion Angles — LENS + Content Data (May 2026)
| Angle | Hook | Why it converts |
|---|---|---|
| "Under-built coffee" | "Your coffee isn't too much. It's under-built." | Reframes the problem — buyer doesn't need to change habits, just upgrade the formula. Zero friction to trial. |
| Ironman/family demand stacking | "My formula has to cover training, work, and being present for Gabriel and Helena." | Real stakes + real context. Not aspirational — relatable to anyone splitting their morning between multiple demands. |
| Red Bull contrast | "Taurine is in Red Bull — but not for the reason you think." | Familiar reference point lowers skepticism. Positions Power Coffee as the clean version of what they already know. |
| Price anchor | "$0.73 a morning vs $5-6 latte" | Always include. Disarms the price objection before it forms. Proven in P6 CTA content. |
| Day 3 proof | "I was really focused on Day 3." (buyer quote) | Specific timeline removes vagueness. Gives the skeptic a concrete milestone to aim for. |

## Voice of Customer — PULSE Data (May 18, 2026)

Real buyer language collected from verified reviews, field interviews, and buyer-facing articles. Use these words — not the brand's words — when writing hooks.

### The #1 Hook Word: "Smooth"
Appears in the only verified public review and across all buyer-facing language. Buyers use it for taste AND for the energy experience. Replace "sustained" with "smooth." Replace "boost" with "level."

### What Buyers Actually Say (quote directly when possible)

**Their pain — use these to open hooks:**
- "Your body's still tired, your head feels heavy"
- "everything just feels a bit off"
- "focus starts slipping or when simple things feel harder than they should"
- "It gave him that first bit of energy, then it wore off"
- "two coffees in and still foggy"

**Their desired state — use these to close hooks:**
- "focus without feeling scattered"
- "things feel more stable"
- "Energy feels more level"
- "no sudden drop that pushes you toward another cup straight away"
- "exactly what I needed"

**Their experience with Power Coffee — direct buyer quotes:**
- "The coffee was rich, smooth, and perfectly flavor— exactly what I needed." — Lauriete Miller, verified buyer
- "I love my morning coffee, but by 2pm I crash. This felt smoother." — Luis D., Go Global interview
- "I was really focused on Day 3." — Luis D., Go Global interview
- "I added it to my oat milk and it worked." — Luis D., Go Global interview
- "The ritual makes it easy." — Luis D., Go Global interview

### Language Map — Swap Brand Words for Buyer Words

| Brand says | Buyer says | Use buyer's version |
|---|---|---|
| "sustained energy" | "level energy" / "smooth energy" | Yes |
| "afternoon crash" | "by 2pm I crash" | Yes — more specific |
| "cognitive clarity" | "focus without feeling scattered" | Yes |
| "boost" / "supercharge" | "no drop" / "holds" | Never use boost/supercharge |
| "optimize" | "fits into my routine" | Yes |
| "mental performance" | "when simple things feel harder than they should" | Use the pain, not the label |

### The Buyer Timeline (critical for onboarding content)
- **Day 1–2:** Buyer expects to be disappointed. Don't lead with hype.
- **Day 3:** First proof moment. *"I was really focused on Day 3"* — build content around this.
- **Week 3:** The real conversion / retention inflection point. Brand's own blog confirms this.
- **Day 430:** Founder still uses it daily. Use as long-term trust signal.

### What NOT to Lead With
- "boost", "supercharge", "maximize", "optimize" — triggers supplement skepticism
- "sustained energy" — brand language, not buyer language
- "biohacking" — buyers don't self-identify this way; it narrows cold audience
- Faith-forward lines — powerful for retention, wrong for cold acquisition hooks

### Audience Gap Confirmed by PULSE
Heavy coffee drinkers who are already caffeinated and still foggy — *"two cups in, still can't get started"* — are a larger and higher-intent segment than current content targets. They are not looking to add coffee; they are looking for their existing coffee to finally work.
