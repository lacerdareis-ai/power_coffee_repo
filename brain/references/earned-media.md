## The five rules

### 1. Editors don't cover products. They cover stories.
Pitching "we launched a new flavor" hands the editor homework. In their words: *"If
you just tell me you're launching a new flavor, I have to do a lot of heavy lifting to
get that into a story."* Do the lifting for them — arrive with the angle already built
and the headline half-written.

**For us:** this is the same rule as our own Desire-Brand principle (open with
identity/feeling, never ingredients) applied to press. The three angles already drafted
in `growth_playbooks/pr_pitch.md` — the AI-operated marketing department, the Ironman
founder, the two-year formulation — are stories. The pouch is not.

### 2. One flavor is rarely news. A trend is.
Thousands of SKUs launch monthly; a single one almost never earns its own piece.
Roundups run constantly. Bucket into a bigger moment and you move from "one-off I'll
skip" to "part of a feature." Editors will even take you naming other brands riding the
same trend — it helps them build the piece.

**For us:** functional coffee / no-crash energy / afternoon-slump is a live category
moment. Pitch into it rather than pitching a pouch. Naming competitors is a feature of
the pitch, not a weakness.

### 3. Relationships beat pitches. Show up before you have news.
Editors remember founders who reach out personally, and especially those they meet in
person — Fancy Food, Expo West, even a farmers market. Be useful without a launch: the
food scientist, the category nerd, the person who is fun to quote. Then you get called
when they need a source.

**For us:** we have a genuinely quotable founder story and an unusual operating angle
(a solo founder running marketing through an AI system, with performance data). That is
source material, not a product pitch.

### 4. Silence is NOT rejection.
Editors receive hundreds of emails daily and cannot reply to most. A good pitch enters a
"mental library" they return to for gift guides, awards and roundups — sometimes months
later. No reply means filed, not refused.

**For us:** `pr_pitch.md` records that no media outreach has happened yet. When it
starts, do not treat silence as a signal to stop or to rewrite the angle. Track sends
and follow the seasonal hooks.

### 5. Discovery moved, and trends expire in days.
TikTok is the engine — one editor called it *"Instagram's chaotic younger sister"* —
alongside Reddit threads, LinkedIn founder journeys and a weekly lap through Trader
Joe's. The catch: a trend that popped last week may already be too late to pitch as new.

**For us:** two direct implications. (a) LinkedIn founder-journey content is an actual
discovery surface for editors, not just for investors — which raises the value of the
Monday founder reel and the "Building Power Coffee" series. (b) Our content cycle is
daily, so we can move on a trend inside its window; our PR cycle cannot. Pitch trends
early or not at all.

## What this changes

- **Pitching:** lead with an angle and a half-written headline, never a SKU.
- **Bucketing:** every pitch should name the larger trend it belongs to.
- **Cadence:** build editor relationships before there is news to share.
- **Patience:** silence is filing, not rejection.
- **Channel:** LinkedIn founder content is doing double duty as press discovery.
