## SCIENCE NOTE — Founder Interview Prep (not for publishing)
**Ingredient / mechanism:** Caffeine — adenosine receptor antagonism

Caffeine's primary mechanism of action is competitive antagonism at adenosine receptors, principally the A1 and A2A subtypes, in the central nervous system. Adenosine is a neuromodulator that accumulates extracellularly across the waking day as a byproduct of cellular energy metabolism; rising adenosine binding at these receptors is a major physiological driver of perceived sleepiness and reduced arousal. Caffeine's molecular structure is sufficiently similar to adenosine that it binds the same receptor sites without activating them, blocking adenosine's inhibitory signal rather than supplying any energy substrate itself. Critically, caffeine does not reduce or clear accumulated adenosine — it only masks receptor-level detection of it for the duration caffeine remains bound, roughly tracking caffeine's own plasma half-life (commonly cited around 5 hours in healthy adults, with meaningful individual variation from genetics, tolerance, and hepatic metabolism). When caffeine clears, the antagonism ends and the underlying, undiminished (or further accumulated) adenosine signal resumes — the mechanistic basis for the "crash" and for diminishing subjective effect across successive doses in a day. At 175mg per serving, Power Coffee's caffeine dose sits within the commonly studied range for alertness and cognitive performance outcomes in human trials. This is a masking/antagonist pathway, not an energy-producing one — a structural distinction worth making explicit to a lay audience who reasonably assume "energy drink" means the product supplies energy.

**Reference:** Fredholm BB, Bättig K, Holmén J, Nehlig A, Zvartau EE. "Actions of caffeine in the brain with special reference to factors that contribute to its widespread use." Pharmacological Reviews, 1999.

**Interview angle:** Caffeine's real mechanism is blocking the brain's detection of a tiredness signal that keeps accumulating the whole time — not producing energy. That reframes "my second cup never hits the same" as expected physiology, not tolerance or a weaker product.
