<h2>Why Taurine Belongs in a Performance Coffee Formula</h2>
<p>Most people recognize taurine as the ingredient that shows up in energy drink labels. Few people know what it actually does. Understanding the mechanism changes how you think about what your morning coffee is capable of delivering.</p>

<h2>What Taurine Actually Does</h2>
<p>Taurine is an amino acid, but not the kind that builds muscle. It functions as an inhibitory neuromodulator — it calms overactive neural pathways without sedating you. When caffeine amplifies neural activity (which is most of what the energy sensation actually is), taurine works in the complementary direction, smoothing the signal and reducing the overstimulation that leads to jitter, anxiety, and the familiar drop when caffeine clears your system.</p>

<h2>What the Research Suggests</h2>
<p>Studies on taurine supplementation at doses of 1–2g daily suggest it may support sustained cognitive performance, help reduce exercise-induced oxidative stress, and contribute to better mitochondrial function under fatigue. One controlled trial found a 1.7% improvement in endurance output at a matched dose — modest in isolation, but meaningful when combined with other performance-supporting ingredients. The research consistently points toward the same mechanism: taurine may help the body and brain sustain output under the kind of prolonged stress that caffeine alone cannot support.</p>

<h2>The Problem with Most Formulas</h2>
<p>Most energy drinks include taurine at 1g — surrounded by 40g of sugar and 200mg of caffeine. The taurine does its job. Everything around it works against it. The result is a spike, a crash, and a reflex reach for the next can by early afternoon.</p>

<h2>How Power Coffee Uses It Differently</h2>
<p>Power Coffee includes 2.07g of taurine per serving — at the high end of the research-supported range. Clean-label, sugar-free, lactose-free. Eleven ingredients working in the same direction.</p>
